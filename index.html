Score notes and data sources
Figure eight
Each day has an approximate solar declination and equation of time. Height selects a note from a C Lydian chord spread across registers. Horizontal position pans that melody and shifts its attack within the beat, so the two axes also leave cues in mono.
Spot voices
Left and right on the disk set stereo position. Vertical position selects a register or chord tone. In pulse and voice modes a larger dark core drops an octave. The pulse mode spreads spots into more attacks on busy days. Tone color adds upper harmonics, while voice mode stacks individual tones.
Harmony
A soft chord tone from each active day rings beneath the next two active days. Its pitch follows a fixed interval pattern within the mode. Changing the starting date or route changes the harmony leading into a day.
Solar images
One NASA SDO/HMI continuum frame per available UTC day, retrieved through Helioviewer near 03:00 UTC, with a midday fallback. The detector finds connected dark cores in a 768-pixel image and keeps their projected positions and sizes. Small spots and features near the limb can be missed. Counts describe what the detector resolved.
Silence
A usable image with zero detections gets one quiet base note. An unavailable or rejected frame gets a full silent beat. Both interrupt sustained harmony.
Travel
The forward route wraps within the 365-date window and visits each date once. The outward route alternates earlier and later dates from the starting point. The close-up stops after 30 positions.
Observation window
This edition uses the archived observations listed above the player.

The pitch choices adapt Matt Russo’s approach to specifying a mapping’s range, direction and scaling. The overlapping chord tones draw on Rick Beato’s use of a repeating voicing within a mode.
